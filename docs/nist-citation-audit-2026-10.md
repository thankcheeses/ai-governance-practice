# NIST AI RMF citation audit — October 2026

Branch `nist-playbook-citations`. Scope: every source in the bank that cites the
NIST AI RMF or its Playbook. All of them live in
`src/content/tracks/aigp-preparation/enrichment.ts`; `questions.json` and
`scenarios.json` carry no `sources` (their only NIST mentions are question text,
options and tags, which this change does not touch). The non-RMF NIST sources
(SP 1270, SP 800-226) and every non-NIST source are unchanged. No question text,
option, key, rationale, distractor note or maintenance record was edited.

## Instruments and how they are cited

| Instrument | Cite form | URL recorded | sourceDate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NIST AI 100-1, *AI RMF 1.0* — Core subcategory outcomes (Tables 1–4) | `NIST AI RMF 1.0 (MEASURE 2.4: …)` | Playbook page for that function, which reproduces the subcategory statement verbatim (`https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/<function>/`); the Playbook root when a cite spans functions | 2023-01-26 |
| NIST AI 100-1 — Parts, Sections and Appendices | `NIST AI RMF 1.0 (Part 2, Section 5.1: …)`, `(Appendix B: …)` | `https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf` | 2023-01-26 |
| NIST AI RMF Playbook — suggested actions, documentation questions, *About* text | `NIST AI RMF Playbook (MEASURE 2.11 suggested actions: …)` | Playbook function page | omitted — the Playbook carries no version date, and its landing page says it is revised periodically |
| NIST AI 600-1, *Generative AI Profile* (used only where AI RMF 1.0 does not reach a generative-specific claim) | `NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile, Section 2.x: …)` | `https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf` | 2024-07-26 |

Every URL above was opened on 2026-10-15 before it was recorded. Section and
appendix wording quoted below was checked against the AI 100-1 PDF; subcategory
statements and Playbook actions were checked against a structured parse of the
Playbook PDF (all 72 subcategories).

Paraphrases follow the subcategory statement, not the question. Where an old
cite paraphrased a statement with words the statement does not contain (for
example "informed by risk rather than by realised loss" for GOVERN 1.5), the
new paraphrase uses NIST's own terms.

### Rationales and the RMF

No rationale misstates what the AI RMF says. Two minor simplifications are
worth the owner's attention (not changed — out of scope for a sources-only
change):

- **aigp-147** (key takeaway: "Measuring quantifies") and **aigp-251**
  (rationale: "Map identifies, Measure quantifies") — AI RMF 1.0 Section 5.3
  says MEASURE "employs quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-method tools,
  techniques, and methodologies". "Quantifies" understates it.
- **aigp-005** (key takeaway: without Govern, "Map, Measure, and Manage have
  nothing to operate within") — consistent with Section 5.1 ("enables the other
  functions") but stronger than it; Section 5 also says that once a governance
  structure is in place the functions "may be performed in any order".

Several items whose sources changed carry a `maintenance.lastReviewed` date.
That record was left as it is: it records a human review, and this change is
not one. The owner may want to re-read those items against the new cites.

### Candidate briefs (proposals for the owner — not added to the bank)

None of these has been written as a question. Per contract §1 and §8 each
needs the owner's judgment that the decision it tests is worth a slot, and any
secondary source named here must be located and read before it is cited.

**1. GOVERN 6.2 — a contingency for a high-risk third-party AI dependency.**
*Judgment tested:* when a consequential process runs on a third-party model the
organization cannot control, governance has to decide in advance what happens
when that model is unavailable, withdrawn or found faulty — a fallback route
(manual or alternative system) and an incident plan that names the third-party
failure — rather than relying on contract remedies after the fact. *Why
distinct:* the bank tests detecting an upstream change (aigp-123), contracting
for notice of it (aigp-225) and who is accountable when it causes harm
(aigp-322); none asks whether the business can keep operating, safely, while the
dependency is down. *Source:* NIST AI RMF 1.0 (GOVERN 6.2: contingency processes
for failures or incidents in high-risk third-party data or AI systems); Playbook
GOVERN 6.2 suggested actions ("consideration of redundancy mechanisms for vital
third-party AI systems"; "Verify that incident response plans address
third-party AI systems").

**2. MANAGE 1.4 — documenting residual risk for downstream acquirers and end
users.** *Judgment tested:* after mitigation, what remains has to be written
down and passed to the people who will acquire or rely on the system, rather
than the risk being recorded internally as "mitigated" and closed. *Why
distinct:* aigp-246 tests a general-purpose provider's duty to document
capabilities and limitations for integrators, and aigp-144 tests removing a
cause versus catching its effects; neither tests what to do with the risk that
is knowingly left after treatment. *Source:* NIST AI RMF 1.0 (MANAGE 1.4:
negative residual risks to downstream acquirers and end users are documented);
a candidate secondary cite is the EU AI Act's residual-risk provisions in the
Article 9 risk management system — to be located and read before use.

**3. MANAGE 2.1 — considering a non-AI alternative.** *Judgment tested:* before
committing resources to managing an AI system's risks, governance should ask
whether a viable non-AI (or non-automated) approach would meet the objective
with less exposure, and record why it was or was not chosen. *Why distinct:*
aigp-105 and aigp-106 test choosing between AI techniques; nothing in the bank
tests whether AI should be used at all. *Source:* NIST AI RMF 1.0 (MANAGE 2.1:
resources required to manage AI risks are taken into account, along with viable
non-AI alternative systems, approaches or methods); Playbook MAP 1.1 suggested
action ("Identify whether there are non-AI or non-technology alternatives that
will lead to more trustworthy outcomes").

**4. MEASURE 3.2 — tracking a risk that cannot yet be measured.** *Judgment
tested:* when no validated metric exists for a risk (for example, erosion of
user autonomy or manipulation through persuasive design), the right response is
to keep it on the register and track it by other means — qualitative review,
expert judgment, incident reports — and document that it is unmeasured, rather
than dropping it because it has no number. *Why distinct:* the bank tests
choosing the right metric (aigp-156, aigp-259) and distrusting a metric that
cannot move (aigp-036, aigp-063), but never the case where no metric exists.
*Source:* NIST AI RMF 1.0 (MEASURE 3.2: risk tracking approaches for settings
where risks are difficult to assess or metrics are not yet available; MEASURE
1.1: risks that will not or cannot be measured are documented); AI RMF 1.0 Part
1, Section 1.2.1 (challenges of measuring risk).

**5. MEASURE 2.12 — environmental impact of training and operation.**
*Judgment tested:* whether the environmental cost of a training or retraining
regime belongs in the assessment and how it is weighed against the benefit —
for example, a proposal to retrain a large model daily where weekly retraining
meets the performance target. *Why distinct:* no question in the bank mentions
energy, emissions or sustainability; aigp-105 treats compute only as cost.
*Source:* NIST AI RMF 1.0 (MEASURE 2.12: environmental impact and
sustainability of AI model training and management activities are assessed and
documented); Playbook MEASURE 2.12 suggested action ("Assess tradeoffs between
AI system performance and sustainable operations"); NIST AI 600-1 Section 2.5
(environmental impacts). The owner should judge whether this is a distinct
governance judgment or only a fact to recall — if the latter, it does not
belong in the bank.

**6. MAP 3.1 — examining the claimed benefit with the same rigor as the risk.**
*Judgment tested:* a go/no-go that accepts a known risk because of an expected
benefit nobody has examined is weighing evidence against assertion; the benefit
needs to be examined and documented before it can justify the risk. *Why
distinct:* aigp-178 resolves a trade-off where both effects were measured;
aigp-304 tests an unsupported performance claim made to buyers; neither tests
an internal approval that rests on an unexamined benefit. *Source:* NIST AI RMF
1.0 (MAP 3.1: potential benefits of intended AI system functionality and
performance are examined and documented; MANAGE 1.1: determination whether the
system achieves its intended purpose and whether deployment should proceed).
